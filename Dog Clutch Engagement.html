The problem

Speeds just after the clutch engages

Shafts A and B are connected by meshing gears of pitch radii rA and rB, so θ̇B/θ̇A = rA/rB. Shaft A and its gear have inertia IA; shaft B and its gear have IB. A third shaft C (inertia IC) is coaxial with B and initially at rest.

Shaft C is suddenly connected to B by engaging the dog clutch. With frictionless bearings, what are the angular velocities of the shafts immediately after engagement?

Worked solution
  1. Let F̂ be the tangential impulse between the gear teeth and Ĵ the impulsive torque transmitted by the clutch. Bearings are frictionless, so they exert no impulsive torque about each shaft's own axis.
  2. Shaft A: IA(θ̇A − ωA) = −F̂ rA
  3. Shaft B (magnitudes): IB(θ̇B − ωB) = F̂ rB − Ĵ. Shaft C: IC θ̇C = Ĵ
  4. Kinematics after engagement: θ̇C = θ̇B = n θ̇A, with n = rA/rB, and before it ωB = n ωA.
  5. Eliminate F̂ and Ĵ (multiply B and C by n and add to A): θ̇A = ωA(IA + n²IB) / (IA + n²(IB + IC)).
  6. Then θ̇B = θ̇C = n θ̇A. B and C turn opposite to A. This is conservation of “generalised momentum” referred to shaft A, not of total angular momentum.
  7. Energy lost fraction: n²IC / (IA + n²(IB + IC)), dissipated in the clutch jaws.
Engineering notes
Impulsive engagement
The clutch acts over a very short time, so speeds change but angles do not. Finite torques (such as drive or friction) have no time to act and are ignored.
Internal impulses
The gear tooth impulse acts equally and oppositely on A and B; the clutch impulse acts equally and oppositely on B and C.
Bearing impulses
The tooth impulse pushes shafts A and B sideways, so their bearings react. These reactions pass through different axes, so they have a net moment about any single x-axis. That is why total angular momentum is not conserved.
Equivalent inertia
Referred to shaft A, inertia on shaft B counts as n² IB. The engagement conserves Ieq θ̇A, like a plastic collision.
